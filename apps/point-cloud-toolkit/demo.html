Decompose

Split a scene that does not fit a model's fixed input budget into chunks.

Select a source to begin.

How a chunk actually gets its points

Three mechanisms decide what a chunk ends up containing, and none of them is visible in the strategy name.

Top-up policy
When a chunk stays under budget, one of four rules fills it: a ball around the centre, a box around the chunk, the bounding box of the k-d cell, or neighbouring Morton ranks. This decides where the overlap comes from geometrically.
Overlap mode
Nine named modes across the families: overlap as a stride of the grid, as a larger cell, as filling to the budget, as a shared split, or as a point threshold. The same number means something different per family.
Halo across scene boundaries
In the thesis, decomposition pulls context from the neighbouring files of a scene and estimates the required margin from a sample. This tool works on exactly one uploaded file and has no neighbouring scenes, so this step is described here rather than offered as a control that would have nothing to point at.

Individual parameters can be overridden as JSON. Accepted keys are exactly those the selected strategy implements.

04

Recompose

Many per-chunk predictions, one scene. The second half of the research question.

  1. A point lies in several chunks
  2. So several predictions exist for it
  3. A merge rule weights them
  4. One class remains

The predictions here do not come from a model. No Point Transformer V3 runs in this tool. Rather than fake real probabilities, a named, seed-fixed generator derives them from the geometry: less certain towards the chunk border, and each chunk with its own fixed confusion. The merge rules computing on them are the canonical ones from the library.
